The hour that states the course's position, before any of the material has to
argue for it.

Most people arrive at a fighter aircraft through its combat record and expect
a course about it to cover combat. This course does not: over twelve weeks it
teaches taking a single-seat jet off the ground, flying a circuit, and
landing it, and no more than that.

The lecture makes the case for that scope in three parts:

- what the aircraft actually is
- why it cannot be flown without a computer
- why a narrower promise than expected is the harder one to keep

It also explains the order of the syllabus. The first practical period
teaches egress — how to get out — not because students are expected to need
it, but because it is the skill with the shortest deadline.

## Reading

The aircraft description chapters of the flight manual, in full. You are not
expected to retain them; you are expected to have seen how much there is.
