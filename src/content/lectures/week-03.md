A system that can refuse you is a system that has authority. The lecture is
about what that means, and about the fact that aviation has been arguing over
it for fifty years without settling it.

Two positions, both held by serious people. The first: the envelope is known,
the consequences of leaving it are known, and a system that prevents you from
leaving it removes an entire category of accident at no cost to anyone flying
competently. The second: the situations in which a pilot needs authority the
designer did not anticipate are, by definition, the situations nobody
anticipated, and a limiter cannot distinguish them from carelessness.

Both positions have accidents attached to them. We will look at examples from
each column, including cases where the protection was correct and the pilot
did not think so, and cases where the pilot was correct and could not get the
authority back.

The lecture then draws the distinction the period will drill: a **limit** is
enforced by the aircraft and you can stop thinking about it; a **restriction**
is written down and enforced by nobody. Almost everything that has killed
people in this type lives in the second column.
