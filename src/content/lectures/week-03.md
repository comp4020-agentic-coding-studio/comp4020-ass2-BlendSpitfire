A system that can refuse you is a system that has authority. The lecture
covers what that means, and the fact that aviation has argued over it for
fifty years without settling it.

Two positions, both held by serious people:

- the envelope is known, the consequences of leaving it are known, and a
  system that prevents you from leaving it removes an entire category of
  accident at no cost to anyone flying competently
- the situations in which a pilot needs authority the designer did not
  anticipate are, by definition, the situations nobody anticipated, and a
  limiter cannot distinguish them from carelessness

Both positions have accidents attached to them. We look at cases from each
column, including one where the protection was correct and the pilot did
not think so, and one where the pilot was correct and could not get the
authority back.

The lecture then draws the distinction the period will drill:

- a **limit** is enforced by the aircraft, and you can stop thinking about it
- a **restriction** is written down and enforced by nobody

Almost everything that has killed people in this type lives in the second
column.
