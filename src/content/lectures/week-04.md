The lecture that turns the rest of the course into arithmetic.

Treat altitude and airspeed as one quantity held in two denominations, and a
sortie becomes a sequence of transactions in it. The engine credits the
account. Drag debits it, continuously, whether or not you are doing anything.
Every manoeuvre is a transfer, and the transfer has a spread.

This framing is not a teaching convenience invented for this course. It came
out of work in the 1960s on comparing fighter aircraft to each other, and the
result was a set of diagrams that let you see, for a given aircraft at a given
weight, exactly what it could and could not afford. Those diagrams changed how
aircraft were specified, which is a reasonable claim to make for a change of
units.

What it gives you here is smaller and more immediately useful: a way of
answering "can I get there from this" without flying it to find out. By week 7
you will fly a circuit that arrives at a chosen point with almost no energy
left, deliberately, and today is where that stops being alarming.

The lecture ends on the manoeuvre students most want to believe in, which is
pulling up when low and slow. We will price it.
