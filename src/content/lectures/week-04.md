The lecture that turns the rest of the course into arithmetic.

Treat altitude and airspeed as one quantity held in two denominations, and
a sortie becomes a sequence of transactions in it:

- the engine credits the account
- drag debits it continuously, whether or not you are doing anything
- every manoeuvre is a transfer, and the transfer has a spread

This framing is not a teaching convenience. It came out of 1960s work
comparing fighter aircraft to each other, producing diagrams that showed,
for a given aircraft at a given weight, exactly what it could and could not
afford. Those diagrams changed how aircraft were specified.

What it gives you here is smaller and more immediately useful: a way of
answering "can I get there from this" without flying it to find out. By
week 7 you fly a circuit that arrives at a chosen point with almost no
energy left, deliberately — today is where that stops being alarming.

The lecture ends on the manoeuvre students most want to believe in, which
is pulling up when low and slow. We will price it.
