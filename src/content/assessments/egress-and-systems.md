## The brief

> Demonstrate that you can get out, and that you know what you are getting out
> of.

Three parts, sat across the end of week 3.

The **egress component** is practical and timed, in the hangar, at the
aircraft. It is not a written description of the sequence. You perform it,
your eyes are closed for part of it, and there is a time.

The **oral** covers the flight control system: what it does with your input,
what its redundancy assumes, and what happens when its computers disagree. You
will be asked at least one question the flight manual does not answer
directly, because the manual's wording is not the same thing as understanding
what it describes.

The **written paper** is short and concerns the distinction from week 3: given
a list of numbers from the operating limitations, say which the aircraft
enforces and which it merely records, and what follows from that difference.

## What you submit

Nothing in advance. All three components are sat in the scheduled slot.

## How it's marked

The egress component carries the most weight because it is the only part of
this examination with a deadline measured in seconds. A correct sequence
performed slowly is not a pass.
