## The period

The stick on your right does not travel. It moves about a quarter of an inch
at full deflection, and the earliest aircraft of this type had a stick that
moved not at all — a pure force transducer, bolted solid, which pilots
disliked enough that a small amount of give was added so that your hand could
believe something had happened.

You are not moving a surface. You are applying a pressure that a computer
reads as a request, and the computer's response arrives faster than your
proprioception can confirm it. This produces the characteristic failure of
this period, which is that you correct, the aircraft has already corrected,
you feel your own correction arrive as an error, and you correct that.

That is a pilot-induced oscillation, and it is worth being precise about the
name: the aircraft is not oscillating. You are. The system is faithfully
executing a series of requests, each of which is a response to the previous
one being answered too well.

The fix is to let go. Not to relax, not to correct more gently — to remove
your hand from the loop entirely and let the aircraft settle, which it will do
in under a second, because it is better at this than you are.

## Afterwards

Nothing written. Sit in the box during an unbooked slot and hold straight and
level for five minutes without correcting anything. Most students cannot do
this on the first attempt.
