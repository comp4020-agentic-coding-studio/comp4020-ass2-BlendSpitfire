## The period

Altitude is stored airspeed. Airspeed is altitude you have not climbed to yet.
There is one quantity — specific energy — and it is held in two denominations
that you can exchange at a rate the aircraft sets, not you.

The engine adds to the account. Drag withdraws from it continuously and
without asking. Everything else you do is a transfer between the two columns,
and the transfer is lossy in one direction only: you can convert altitude to
speed almost perfectly, and speed to altitude rather less so.

This is why the answer to being low and slow is not "pull up". Pulling up
spends the one denomination you are short of, to buy the other one you are
also short of, at a poor rate, while drag charges you for the privilege of
asking. The manoeuvre feels like a solution and is a second problem.

By week 7 you will be flying a circuit, which is a sequence of deliberate
energy decisions ending with almost none left at a point you chose in advance.
Today is where that arithmetic starts.
