## Before you arrive

Your six covered-indicator circuits from week 7, with the resulting airspeeds.

## The period

Touchdown is not the end of the approach. It is the beginning of a
deceleration that you are going to fly, with the same wing, on the ground.

Hold the nose up after the main wheels are down and the aircraft becomes a
large airbrake: the wing that was lifting is now mostly drag, and it will slow
you far more effectively than the wheel brakes will at that speed. Hold it
there. There is a speed below which the elevator can no longer keep the nose
up, and if you wait for the nose to fall by itself it arrives hard.

So the aerobrake has a beginning you choose and an end the aircraft chooses,
and your job is to lower the nose deliberately a moment before it would have
come down anyway.

The second half of the period is the progression check. It is flown to the
published tolerances, it is not negotiable, and it has no fixed number of
attempts. Tomas would rather sign it in week 9 than sign it early.

## Afterwards

If you passed, the authorisation for week 10 goes to Wren for signature. If
you did not, book the box. Nobody flies the aircraft without this signature,
and there is no version of this course where that rule bends.
