## Before you arrive

Your specific energy figures from week 4, for each corner of the circuit. You
will fly against your own arithmetic today.

## The period

The circuit is a rectangle: upwind, crosswind, downwind, base, final. It
exists so that an approach can be flown from a known geometry rather than
improvised from wherever you happen to be, and it is the single most
repetitive thing in this course. You will fly it until it is boring, and then
past that.

The substantive content is on final, and it concerns which instrument you are
looking at.

The speed that keeps a wing flying is not a fixed number. It changes with
weight, with fuel burned, with how hard you are turning. What does *not*
change is the angle at which that wing stops flying. So the approach is flown
on angle of attack — on an indexer showing three lights, of which you want the
middle one — and the airspeed that results is a consequence you do not have to
compute.

Students find this uncomfortable, because the airspeed indicator is right
there, reading a number, and the number feels like information. Today it is a
distraction. By week 8 you will not look at it on final at all.

## Afterwards

Fly six circuits in a booked slot with the airspeed indicator covered. Note
what your airspeed turned out to be on each one, and how little it varied.
