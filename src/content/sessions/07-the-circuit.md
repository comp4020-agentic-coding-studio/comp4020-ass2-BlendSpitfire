## Before you arrive

Your specific energy figures from week 4, for each corner of the circuit. You
will fly against your own arithmetic today.

## In this period

The circuit is a rectangle: upwind, crosswind, downwind, base, final. It lets
an approach be flown from a known geometry rather than improvised, and it is
the most repetitive part of the course — flown until it is boring, then past
that.

The substantive content is on final, and concerns which instrument you are
looking at:

- the speed that keeps a wing flying changes with weight, fuel burned and
  turn rate
- the angle at which the wing stops flying does not change

For that reason final is flown on angle of attack — on an indexer showing
three lights, of which you want the middle one — and the resulting airspeed
is a consequence you do not have to compute.

Students find this uncomfortable, because the airspeed indicator reads a
number that feels like information. By week 8 it is not looked at on final at
all.

## Afterwards

Fly six circuits in a booked slot with the airspeed indicator covered. Note
what your airspeed turned out to be on each one, and how little it varied.
